Bond Dipole Moment Database

Average bond moments, aromatic group moments and reference molecular moments, in debye (1 D = 3.336 × 10⁻³⁰ C·m). Each value lists its source set, so you can see how much the published averages differ from one table to another.

Sign convention. Bond moments are given as magnitudes, with the negative end on the more electronegative atom (written A⁺–B⁻). The C–H moment is set by convention: these tables assume C⁻–H⁺. Group moments follow the Williams sign convention: negative means the group is the negative end of the molecule (electron-withdrawing) and positive means it is electron-donating, relative to H = 0.

For a diatomic, the measured molecular moment is the bond moment. These are gas-phase experimental magnitudes. The sign is not given, so the "δ⁻ end" column is only the electronegativity prediction (CO is the classic exception). μe = equilibrium value, μ0 = ground vibrational state. MW = microwave, MB = molecular beam, DR = double resonance. Open-shell, excited-state and ion rows are flagged; for comparing bond polarities, use the closed-shell ground states.

Diatomic dipole moment vs electronegativity difference

39 closed-shell, ground-state main-group diatomics from the CCCBDB experimental list. Hover a point for details.

Two regimes stand out. The alkali compounds (circles) have moments of 6–11 D, a large fraction of what a full electron charge separated by the bond length would give, and they rise with bond length (Li → K, F → I) more than with Δχ. The p-block molecules (triangles) stay below about 3.5 D, and within a family μ tracks Δχ only roughly: CO has Δχ = 0.89 but μ = 0.11 D, with the polarity reversed (C⁻O⁺). Δχ predicts the direction of polarity much better than its size. Bond lengths aren't in this dataset, so percent ionic character (μ / e·r) isn't plotted.

    Using these values

    • The Diatomics tab leaves out homonuclear molecules (μ = 0) and the transition-metal species in the CCCBDB list (ScH, CuO, CuF).
    • Bond moments are additive only approximately. Induction between neighboring polar bonds is folded into the averages, so CH₃Cl (1.87 D) and CHCl₃ (1.01 D) can't both be reproduced from one fixed pair of C–H and C–Cl values.
    • Lone pairs aren't assigned separately, so their contribution is absorbed into the bond values for O–H, N–H, C–O and so on.
    • Hybridization matters: C(sp)–H and C(sp²)–X differ from the sp³ averages listed here.
    • Group moments, which were fitted to monosubstituted benzenes, usually predict disubstituted benzenes better than summing individual bond moments does.
